贴满五厘米吸音棉，五十赫兹照样轰；墙角摆两块成品小方盒，低频几乎没动。低频陷阱失效通常只有两个原因：厚度不够，位置不对。

## 吸声看波长，不看面积

五十赫兹的波长接近七米，四分之一波长也有一米七。多孔材料要到厚度接近四分之一波长才明显起作用，十厘米的棉从三四百赫兹往下就迅速失效。墙角两面墙的压力叠在一起，同样厚度在那里最有效；留空腔等于加厚，二十到三十厘米的角落堆叠能下探到八九十赫兹。

## 四种做法与它们的频段

| 做法 | 典型厚度 | 起效范围 | 占地方 | 适合 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 多孔棉板 | 5–10 厘米 | 300 Hz 以上 | 小 | 中高频与颤动回声 |
| 厚棉加空腔 | 20–30 厘米 | 80–150 Hz | 中 | 墙角、后墙 |
| 膜或板共振 | 10–20 厘米 | 60–200 Hz | 中 | 前墙局部 |
| 亥姆霍兹 | 按调谐定 | 窄带 | 小 | 单一顽固峰 |

表里的范围是起点，不是保证。同一块材料贴墙与离墙十厘米，低频吸收差出一大截；厂家标称的吸声系数多在混响室测得，与墙角条件并不相同。

## 位置：压力最大处

低频在刚性墙面压力最大、空气速度最小，所以陷阱要贴在墙角、墙与天花板的交线、以及音箱后方。四角全做比只做两只前角均匀；只能先做两处时，选音箱后方那两只，有余力再补听音位后方一只。房间接近正方形时模式挤在一起，角落处理要更厚。

## 陷阱救不了的情形

一侧是轻质隔墙或玻璃，低频从墙里漏走，角落吸声改变不了漏声造成的谷。长宽比过于极端时模式稀疏，再厚的陷阱也填不平，只能改摆位或改用途。把陷阱当隔声材料是另一类错误：吸声降低室内混响，不阻止声音传给邻居。

::: en

Five centimetres of foam on every surface, and 50 Hz still booms; two shop-bought corner blocks, and the bottom octave hardly moves. Bass traps fail for two reasons: not enough depth, or the wrong position.

## Absorption follows wavelength, not area

A 50 Hz wave is nearly seven metres long, so a quarter of it is about 1.7 metres. Porous material only works seriously when its depth approaches that quarter wavelength, and ten centimetres of fibre gives up below roughly 300–400 Hz. Corners stack the pressure of two walls, which is why the same thickness does more there; an air gap acts as extra depth, and 20–30 cm of corner fill reaches the 80–90 Hz region.

## Four constructions and the band each covers

| Type | Typical depth | Working range | Floor space | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Porous panel | 5–10 cm | Above 300 Hz | Small | Midrange and flutter echo |
| Thick fill with gap | 20–30 cm | 80–150 Hz | Medium | Corners, rear wall |
| Membrane or panel | 10–20 cm | 60–200 Hz | Medium | Front wall, in patches |
| Helmholtz | Tuned | Narrow band | Small | One stubborn peak |

Those ranges are a starting point, not a promise. The same material against the wall and ten centimetres off it absorbs very differently at the bottom, and published coefficients come from reverberation chambers rather than a real corner.

## Put them where pressure peaks

At a rigid boundary the low-frequency pressure is highest and air velocity is lowest, so traps belong in corners, at wall-ceiling junctions and behind the speakers. Treating all four corners is more even than only the front pair; with room for two, take the ones behind the speakers and add one behind the listener. A near-square room crowds its modes together and needs thicker corner treatment.

## Where a trap cannot help

If one side is a stud wall or glazing, bass leaves through it, and corner absorption cannot fill the resulting dip. A very extreme length-to-width ratio spreads modes so thinly that no practical trap evens them out; change the layout or the room's use instead. Treating a trap as soundproofing is the other common error: absorption shortens the decay inside, it does not stop sound reaching the neighbours.
