::: zh
合唱团不会因为写了七个声部就自动变厚。声部数量、换气点和歌词重音，任何一项没落对，排练时都会退回三部和声。

## 歌词先定节奏

先读一遍词，标出重音与句读，再配旋律。一个音节配一个音，还是拉长成花腔，决定声部能不能同时换气。中文歌词还要看声调：旋律上行落在去声字上，唱出来会像另一个字。英文的辅音群要留时间，否则合唱团会把辅音唱成一团。词在前，和声在后，顺序反了就得重写。

## 四个声部的舒适区

女高音不是越高越好，长音停在 G5 以上会紧；女低音不是女高音低八度，G3 以下容易虚；男高音的高音区需要提前准备，别把整段旋律挂在那里；男低音在 C2 以下只剩气声。分声部时先看这两端的音，再排内声部。四部写不下时先减音，不要随手加 divisi。

| 声部 | 常用音域 | 换气 | 混音处理 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 女高音 | C4 到 A5 | 每句留一口气 | 少量高频激励 |
| 女低音 | G3 到 E5 | 内声部易被盖 | 中低频收一点 |
| 男高音 | C3 到 A4 | 高音前留半拍 | 二到四千赫兹稍退 |
| 男低音 | E2 到 E4 | 长音最耗气 | 八十到一百二十赫兹控制 |

## 换气点写进谱

换气记号不是建议，是编配的一部分。一句超过四小节，女高音和男低音都会偷偷换气，位置还不一样。长音可以用声部交替呼吸，但要在谱上标明谁在什么时候换。休止不是空白，是下一句的准备时间。[[glossary:motet|经文歌]]这类连续文本尤其要算好气口。

## 语言与元音

合唱的融合靠元音，清晰靠辅音。同一个和弦，唱 ah 比唱 ee 更容易融在一起；闭口元音在高音区会挤。写外语作品时，把发音说明或国际音标附在谱上，比让合唱团猜更省时间。中文作品要先决定用普通话还是方言，两者的声调与咬字完全不同。[[glossary:mass|弥撒]]的拉丁文有固定的句读，改写之前先确认教会传统。

## 混音救不了写作

很多采样合唱只录了有限几个元音，写快速辅音会露出拼接；真实录音里，混响太多会把歌词吞掉，太少又显得干。混音能修平衡，修不了换气点。若一段合唱听起来不齐，先检查谱上的气口与句读，再动均衡与压缩。

::: en
A choir does not get thicker because you wrote seven parts. Part count, breath points and word stress decide whether the rehearsal keeps the harmony or falls back to three parts.

## The words set the rhythm

Read the text aloud, mark the stresses and the punctuation, then write the melody. One note per syllable or a long melisma decides whether the parts can breathe together. In Mandarin, the tone matters as well: a rising phrase under a falling tone can turn the word into another one. English consonant clusters need time, or the choir sings them as one blur. Words first, harmony second; reversing the order means rewriting.

## Comfortable ranges

A soprano is not better the higher she goes; a sustained note above G5 tightens. An alto is not a soprano an octave down; below G3 the tone goes hollow. The tenor's top needs preparing, so do not hang a whole melody there, and a bass below C2 is mostly air. Check both outer ends before filling in the inner parts. If four parts will not fit, remove notes rather than adding divisi.

| Part | Range | Breath | Mix treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soprano | C4 to A5 | One breath per phrase | A little high-end lift |
| Alto | G3 to E5 | Inner part easily covered | Trim the low mids |
| Tenor | C3 to A4 | Half a beat before the top | Ease back 2 to 4 kHz |
| Bass | E2 to E4 | Long notes cost most air | Control 80 to 120 Hz |

## Write the breaths in

A breath mark is part of the arrangement, not a suggestion. Past four bars, sopranos and basses will steal breaths in different places. Sustained notes can use staggered breathing, but the score has to say who breathes when. A rest is not empty space; it is preparation for the next phrase. Continuous texts such as a motet need the breath plan worked out first.

## Language and vowels

Blend comes from vowels, clarity from consonants. On the same chord, ah fuses more easily than ee, and closed vowels pinch in the high register. For a foreign text, put a pronunciation guide or IPA on the score instead of leaving the choir to guess. For Chinese, decide between Mandarin and a dialect before writing; the tones and diction are different. Latin mass settings have traditional punctuation, so check the convention before rewriting it.

## Mixing cannot fix the writing

Many sampled choirs record only a few vowels, and fast consonants expose the joins. In a real recording, too much reverb swallows the words and too little sounds dry. A mix can fix balance but not breath points. If the choir sounds unsteady, check the rests and punctuation before touching EQ or compression.
